# Double Bind Trainer — UX Review & Learning Feedback (2026-11-23)

## Diagnosis
The former trainer jumped immediately to the next dialogue after a correct choice. The learner saw no explicit acknowledgement of what the answer demonstrated, could not reconstruct the evidence, and had no durable view of how the trap changed. Incorrect answers displayed a vague hint with no obvious recovery.

## New contract
1. **Conversation first.** No onboarding form or progress meter.
2. **Try a response.** Selection is retained; the next conversation does not replace the current one automatically.
3. **Explain the reasoning.** Every correct response displays the selected interpretation, supporting utterance (expandable), its significance for the dialogue, what changes, and a clearly labeled continuation action.
4. **Not stuck.** Incorrect response displays why the frame is incomplete and offers either a retry or a guided reveal; no countdown, penalty or forced reset.
5. **Map the process.** A compact diagram distinguishes two competing demands, the blocked option of discussing their contradiction, and a possible opening at the rule/communication level. In the final summary the frame is visible again.
6. **No false inevitability.** Finding “AND” is not mandatory. Sometimes a limit, safe distance or exit from a harmful interaction is appropriate.
7. **Context available.** The initial client conversation can be expanded on later steps. Theory remains optional, while the main interaction uses ordinary Hebrew.
8. **No numerical progress.** The context labels identify what we are currently trying to learn without suggesting a linear clinical cure.

## Why the intervention is at another level
Original double bind formulation (Bateson et al., 1956): contradictory injunctions and a constraint against escaping or metacommunicating. The instructional move is to distinguish the **content of two commands** from the **rule that prohibits negotiating their relation**. As an educational intervention, questioning the rule can open a new option. This does not imply the original hypothesis about schizophrenia has been established.

Example (fictional): “If I disagree with Dad, I am disrespectful; if I obey, I betray the kind of father I want to be; talking about this conflict itself is disrespectful.” The target is not “convince Dad he is wrong” but “does respect necessarily mean agreement?” The answer can be a new distinction, a boundary, or an alternative behavior; it does not guarantee reconciliation.

Dialectical validation (DBT): both sides may carry partial meanings worth hearing, without treating every claim as equally true or excusing coercion. Somatic checks are subjective signals, not proof of truth.

## Educational design support
- Hattie & Timperley (2007), *The Power of Feedback*, Review of Educational Research 77(1): 81–112, https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487. Feedback should address goals, current interpretation and the next action. Application: our “what you saw / why it matters / what happens next” sequence.
- Nielsen Norman Group (2024 review), *10 Usability Heuristics*, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/. Application: action outcomes are explicitly visible; recovery is possible; original conversation can be revisited; interface terms match learners’ language.
- Bateson, Jackson, Haley & Weakland (1956), *Toward a theory of schizophrenia*, Behavioral Science 1(4):251–264, https://doi.org/10.1002/bs.3830010402. Application: diagram explicitly separates contradictory demands from block on metacommunication.
- The above research supports the rationale for clarity and conceptual distinctions; the combined educational trainer has not itself been clinically validated.
