# Chapter 1: The question that works (editorial draft)

A client says: “I always destroy every important relationship.” A Meta Model practitioner can spot the generalization in “always,” the vague action in “destroy,” and the missing reference in “every important relationship.” But a grammatically valid challenge is not necessarily the most useful intervention.

The first question is not “Which violation can I repair?” but “What is the person trying to communicate, what are we trying to understand, and what might the next question do to the relationship?” The same sentence may invite factual clarification, an expression of distress, an attempt to test the relationship, or a wish to examine an old conclusion.

Instead of reflexively saying “Always?”, we might ask, with permission: “When you say ‘destroy,’ what happened in the most recent situation?” Or: “Do you want us to understand what happened first, or explore whether ‘always’ fits every relationship?” Both questions can help, but they do different work.

Training goal: distinguish a valid pattern question from a clinically appropriate question. A useful question is one the speaker is willing and able to answer, that serves an agreed purpose, and whose answer can genuinely change the practitioner's hypothesis.
